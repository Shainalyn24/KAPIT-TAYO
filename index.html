1 · Spot it

Saan nangyayari? Kahit saan.

One law, the Safe Spaces Act (RA 11313), covers all three places. Open each one.14

On the street and in public

What it looks like

  • Catcalling and whistling
  • Unwanted invitations
  • Sexist or hateful insults
  • Touching or groping
  • Flashing

All are listed in the Act.3

What the law says

It is a crime in streets, jeepneys, malls and other public places.14

A first offense can mean a fine starting at PHP 1,000 plus community service. Serious acts can mean jail or a PHP 30,000 fine.1

What to do

Tell the staff of the place, the police or your local hotline. They must call the police and share CCTV footage when a court asks.2

Online

What it looks like

  • Online stalking
  • Nonstop unwanted messages
  • Sexual or sexist threats and insults
  • Sharing someone's private photos or videos

Listed in the Act.14

What the law says

The penalty is a fine of PHP 100,000 to PHP 500,000, jail time, or both.5

Private messages count too.1

What to do

Take screenshots first. Then report to the PNP Anti-Cybercrime Group.5 Its AlengPulis helpline is for women and children.13

At school and at work

What it looks like

  • Unwanted sexual comments or touching
  • Asking for sexual favors
  • Behavior that hurts a person's dignity

Covered by the Act.115

What the law says

The 2019 law widened the old 1995 law.15

Bosses and school heads can be punished if they ignore a report.4

What to do

Tell HR or the school committee (CODI). Employers and schools must act on your report.5

Five laws, one goal: no more bastos

Tap a law to see what it is about and how it connects to this site.

RA 11313Safe Spaces Act (Bawal Bastos Law), 2019

What it is about

Defines and punishes gender-based sexual harassment in streets and public spaces, online, workplaces and schools. It covers strangers and peers, not only people in authority, and tells local governments to run a hotline.3

How it connects here

This is the backbone of this site. Every place you opened, every penalty and the 612 reports to the PCW all come from this law.117

Think of it when

Catcalling, unwanted remarks, groping, flashing, online stalking and threats, even if the person says it was a joke.

RA 7877Anti-Sexual Harassment Act, 1995

What it is about

The older law. It punishes sexual harassment in work, education and training settings, mainly when the harasser holds power over you, like a boss or teacher.

How it connects here

The 2019 law widened it, so peers and strangers can be held responsible too. This links to the school and work topic.15

Think of it when

A superior pressures you for sexual favors, or an office or school ignores your report.

RA 9262Anti-Violence Against Women and Their Children Act, 2004

What it is about

Protects women and their children from physical, sexual, psychological and economic abuse by a husband, ex, live-in or dating partner. It allows protection orders.

How it connects here

Harassment is not always from a stranger. Stalking, threats and nasty messages from an ex can fall here, and the PNP Anti-Cybercrime Group applies this law online.16

Think of it when

A current or former partner keeps messaging, tracking or threatening you.

RA 9995Anti-Photo and Video Voyeurism Act, 2009

What it is about

Punishes taking photos or videos of private or sexual acts or body parts without consent, and copying or sharing them, even if the person first agreed to be recorded.21

How it connects here

This is the law behind the voyeurism numbers in The numbers: 294 to 347 cases from 2023 to 2024.18 It also matters for leaked private photos.

Think of it when

Someone secretly records you, or shares your private photos or videos after a breakup.

RA 9710Magna Carta of Women, 2009

What it is about

A women’s human rights law. It commits the State to end discrimination against women and to protect them from violence.

How it connects here

It is the bigger promise behind all of these laws: being safe in public and online is a right, not a favor.

Think of it when

You want to understand why the State must act, or push your school or barangay to do more.

4 · Hindi ka nag-iisa

What to do: Incident action guide

Follow these three steps, then use the private note-taker to write down details before you call or file a blotter.

1

Ensure safety first

Move to a safe, populated area, alert nearby security officers, or call 911 if you are in immediate danger.22

2

Preserve evidence

Take screenshots of DMs and posts. Note the date, time, vehicle plate numbers, or names of eyewitnesses.5

3

File an official report

Report to the Barangay VAW Desk, PNP Aleng Pulis, the PNP Anti-Cybercrime Group, or your school or work CODI.51322

In the 2016 survey, half of the women who were harassed did nothing, and some were too afraid.6 Believe them. Never blame a victim for what they wore.9
